1. Purpose

Jot is a pocketable device for capturing spoken notes. The user holds a side button, speaks, and releases. The recording is stored on the device first, then transferred to the iPhone app over Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE). The app transcribes the audio on the phone and turns it into notes, tasks, reminders, or calendar events. This document describes the Rev A custom PCB: what is on the board, how the blocks connect, and why each part was chosen.

4. Block descriptions

4.1 USB-C input

HRO TYPE-C-31-M-12 receptacle. Supplies 5 V for charging and carries USB data to the nRF52840. Two 5.1 kΩ resistors on the CC pins tell a USB-C charger to supply power; without them many chargers supply nothing. A USBLC6-2SC6 ESD protection part sits on the data lines because the connector is the part people touch. The module's VBUS pin is fed through a 2.2 Ω series resistor (R14), as the nRF52840 Product Specification recommends for new designs, with 10 µF to ground.

4.2 Charger

TI BQ25101 single-cell LiPo charger. It stops at 4.2 V. A 680 Ω ISET resistor sets the charge current to about 200 mA (KISET 135 AΩ / 680 Ω), which is 0.5C for the 400 mAh cell. TS goes to ground through 10 kΩ, TI's connection when no thermistor is used. The open-drain CHG pin drives the green charge LED directly, so charging is visible even if the firmware is not running. The system load runs from the battery rail, as on the XIAO nRF52840.

4.3 LiPo battery

Adafruit 3898: one 3.7 V, 400 mAh lithium-polymer cell (about 36 × 17 × 7.8 mm) with its own protection circuit and a JST-PH lead. This is above the 150 to 350 mAh range first expected, so its fit must be confirmed when the case is designed. Check the lead polarity against the J2 footprint before first power-up: JST-PH polarity is not consistent between battery vendors.

4.4 Fuel gauge (MAX17048)

Estimates battery percentage from the cell voltage using a battery model, with no current-sense resistor. It is powered from the battery, reports over I2C (address 0x36), and draws only a few microamps, so it can stay on all the time. Its ALRT output can wake the nRF52840 on low battery.

4.5 3.3 V LDO

TI TPS7A0233 (200 mA, 25 nA quiescent current, 270 mV maximum dropout at 200 mA) converts the battery voltage (about 3.0 to 4.2 V) to 3.3 V for the module, flash, accelerometer, and mic switch, with 1 µF on its input and output. Below about 3.5 V on the battery the rail follows the battery down; every 3.3 V part works down to at least 2.7 V. The nRF52840's internal high-voltage regulator (REG0) is not used: it starts at 1.8 V until firmware programs UICR.REGOUT0, which the 2.7 V minimum flash cannot run from, and it allows only about 25 mA for external loads, which a flash write (up to 25 mA) plus LEDs can exceed.

4.6 nRF52840 module

Raytac MDBT50Q-1MV2: a pre-certified module with chip antenna, so the RF design is already done and tested. It runs in normal voltage mode, following Raytac reference circuit 8.3: VDD and VDDH are tied to the 3.3 V rail with 10 µF, and DCCH is left unconnected, so no REG0 inductor is needed. A Seiko SC-32S 32.768 kHz crystal (12.5 pF load, the maximum the nRF52840 supports) uses two 18 pF load capacitors, from the nRF52840 PS formula (section 5.4.2.4) C = 2·CL − Cpin − Cpcb = 2 × 12.5 − 4 (typical pin capacitance) − 3 (estimated board stray) = 18 pF. The stray is an estimate; check the LFXO frequency on the built board. P0.18 is kept as a pin reset on a test pad, alongside the SWD pads. The antenna end of the module goes at the board edge with no copper underneath, and away from the battery and the user's hand.

4.7 Audio

Mic 1 is a TDK T3902 bottom-port PDM MEMS microphone (64 dBA SNR, 1.65 to 3.63 V). It replaced the planned Infineon IM69D130 (69 dB SNR) because the T3902 is stocked at LCSC. Bottom port means the sound enters through a hole in the PCB under the mic; this fixes which board face points at the case opening. Mic 1's SELECT pin is grounded (right channel). A footprint for Mic 2 shares the same two PDM wires with SELECT tied high (left channel) and is not populated on Rev A (DNP). Both mics and the Rec LED are powered from a switched rail: an AO3401A P-MOSFET with a 100 kΩ gate pull-up, so the rail is off by default and on when the GPIO drives low. Firmware should release the PDM clock pin while the rail is off.

4.8 Storage

Winbond W25Q01JVZEIQ: 1 Gbit (128 MB) NOR flash on QSPI, WSON-8 8 × 6 mm, quad mode enabled at the factory. It is two stacked 512 Mbit dies with linear 4-byte addressing; each die reports its own BUSY bit. Recordings are written here first and deleted once the phone confirms it has them.

4.9 Sensors on I2C

LIS2DW12 low-power accelerometer (address 0x19, SA0 tied high: its internal pull-up cannot be switched off, so tying it low would draw about 150 µA): detects raise-to-wake, double-tap, and shake in hardware and signals the nRF52840 on an interrupt pin, so the main chip can stay asleep. DRV2605L haptic driver (address 0x5A), powered from the battery with 1 µF on VDD and on REG, drives a Vybronics VG0832012 LRA (8 mm coin, 235 Hz, 1.8 Vrms) for crisp taps from a built-in effect library. Both share the I2C bus with the fuel gauge; one pair of 4.7 kΩ pull-ups serves the whole bus.

4.10 UI

Side button: TS-1010-C-A right-angle tactile switch to ground, using the internal pull-up; it is also the wake source. Rec LED: red 0603 on the mic rail through 1 kΩ. RGB status LED: common-anode 1615 RGB with the anode on the battery rail. Each cathode is switched to ground by an AO3400A (gate from a PWM pin, 100 kΩ pull-down) through 1 kΩ (red) and 560 Ω (green, blue), about 2 mA at 3.7 V. Green and blue need about 2.6 V at 2 mA (up to about 3.0 V worst case), and the 3.3 V rail falls to about 3.0 V as the battery empties, so driving them from a 3.3 V pin left no headroom whatever the resistor; from the battery they stay lit down to 3.0 V, and no GPIO sees the battery voltage. Draft 2 drove a common-cathode TJ-S1615 from GPIOs through 100 Ω, which does not fix the headroom. Buzzer: MLT-5020 magnetic buzzer, 4 kHz, from the battery rail through an AO3400A low-side MOSFET with a 1N4148W flyback diode, for confirmation tones and a find-my-Jot feature.

4.11 NFC

The nRF52840 has an NFC tag built in. Rev A adds a coil antenna drawn in copper on the PCB and two equal tuning capacitors (C0G, 0402) from NFC1 and NFC2 to ground, forming an LC tank tuned to 13.56 MHz. Tapping Jot to the iPhone can open the app or start pairing. The tuning follows Nordic white paper nWP-026 (in Datasheets/NFC/):

Ctune = 2 / ((2π · 13.56 MHz)2 · Lant) − Cp − Cint,   Cint ≈ 4 pF

Coil inductance Lant1.0 µH1.5 µH2.0 µH2.5 µH
Starting Ctune (Cp = 0)~270 pF~180 pF~134 pF~106 pF

Steps: (1) draw the coil once the board outline is known and calculate its inductance from its dimensions; (2) fit the estimated capacitor; (3) tune on the built board with a network analyzer (S11 dip at 13.56 MHz; lower C if the dip is above, raise it if below). Avoid the NFC Forum reference poller sizes (46 × 32 mm and 25 × 20 mm) to prevent overcoupling.

4.12 Power budget (estimate)

First-pass estimates from the component datasheets (typical values at 25 °C), to be replaced by measurements at bring-up.

ModeCurrent (typ)Main contributors
Standby (System OFF)≈10 to 17 µADRV2605L shutdown 4 µA, MAX17048 hibernate 3 µA, cell protection ≤7 µA (max), flash deep power-down 1 µA, LIS2DW12 low-power 1 µA, nRF52840 System OFF 0.4 µA
Recording (Opus, DC/DC on)≈6.2 mA (≈9.2 mA with DC/DC off)CPU 3.3 mA, Rec LED ≈1.3 mA, flash program and erase ≈0.6 mA, PDM ≈0.5 mA (assumed), mic 0.43 mA
BLE sync≈8 mA for about 1 min a dayRadio TX at 0 dBm 6.4 mA, RX 6.3 mA

With 320 mAh usable (80% of 400 mAh), the electronics alone allow over two years of standby, so the cell's own self-discharge sets the real limit and R5 is met with a wide margin. A typical day (ten one-minute recordings and one sync) uses about 1.6 mAh, roughly 200 days per charge (about 150 days with the DC/DC regulator off). Firmware must put the flash into deep power-down before System OFF (in standby it can draw up to 170 µA), enable the DC/DC regulator, and keep the haptic driver's EN pin low.

4.13 Bring-up and test plan

Check the battery lead polarity, then feed the battery input from a bench supply at 3.7 V with a 50 mA current limit. Confirm the 3.3 V rail, that idle current is in the microamp range, and that nothing runs warm. Then, in order: probe the LDO output and the module's VDD; flash a blinky over SWD and check reset; scope the 32.768 kHz crystal once firmware starts it; read the flash JEDEC ID over QSPI, then write and read back across both dies; find the three I2C parts (0x19, 0x36, 0x5A); switch on the mic rail, confirm the Rec LED, and capture and play back PDM audio; pair over BLE and transfer a clip; test USB charging and the UF2 bootloader. Finally, measure every mode on the battery input with a power profiler (Nordic PPK2) and update section 4.12 with real numbers.
